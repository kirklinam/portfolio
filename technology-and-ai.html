Technology & AI

Facilitating district AI strategy, engineering a live student-data infrastructure in Google Apps Script, and building a gamified calculator-fluency platform — technology built to amplify the teacher, never replace them.

Dallas ISD · January 2026

Presenting at Dallas ISD AI Forum

On January 5, 2026, I was selected to facilitate a small group discussion at the Dallas ISD AI Math Forum, leading approximately 15 middle school math teachers from across the district in a conversation about AI teaching assistance. The forum brought together educators navigating the same question from very different starting points: how to use AI tools to extend teacher capacity without compromising instructional judgment or student thinking.

My facilitation drew directly from my own classroom practice. I shared how I use AI as an infrastructure tool, generating differentiated practice materials, drafting individualized student feedback at scale, and automating the data pipelines that let me spend planning time on instruction instead of spreadsheets. The discussion centered on a distinction I hold firmly: AI should amplify the teacher's voice and reach, never replace the relational and diagnostic work that only a teacher can do. Teachers in my group left with concrete entry points matched to their comfort levels, from prompt techniques for generating STAAR-aligned items to fully automated feedback systems, including ways to use the district-adopted Sibme app for coaching and feedback regarding teacher talk-time, behavior, and lesson clarity. Greiner · STAAR Review Season

Creating Student Progress Dashboards Through Google Apps Script

During STAAR review season at Greiner, I needed every one of my students to know exactly where they stood, what they had earned, and what to do next, every single day. No existing district tool offered that, so I built it. Using Google Apps Script, I engineered a two-sided dashboard system: each student received an individual Progress Portal spreadsheet showing their weekly skills, SmartScore targets, verified written work, points earned, and personalized feedback, while I managed everything from a central teacher tracker that could pull data from and push data to over 100 individual student files across four class periods with a single menu click.

The system grew into a full data infrastructure. It automatically located and matched each student's portal file in Google Drive, pulled their self-reported IXL SmartScores, then audited those scores against actual IXL exports to correct inflated entries and credit unreported progress. It calculated weekly points through a transparent rubric, generated individualized feedback drafts that I reviewed and approved before pushing them to each student's "Mr. Linam's Corner," produced parent-ready progress messages, and built a live grades tab on every portal showing students exactly how to raise their grade with specific point values attached to each option. A countdown to STAAR day pushed to every portal kept urgency visible.

Shown above are the teacher back-end versions of students' progress portal. Any data students enter from IXL Progress automatically populates in what I called the "Birds Eye Tracker." When students failed to enter their progress, a simple export from IXL pasted into the sheet would automatically update the student and teacher portals anyway.

Shown below is the student view. They had the option to assign as many skills as they deemed necessary based on assessment data and confidence levels. The dashboard-like feel motivated students to keep up with updating their progress and add more skills under their belt before the state assessment.

The most sophisticated layer came in the final weeks before the test, when I differentiated the entire system into three cohorts based on student data, each receiving different skill assignments, different targets, and different point structures, all rebuilt automatically onto each student's portal based on a single cohort label. Students checked their portals daily, and written work verification became a one-click sync between my review sheet and their dashboards. The system turned data tracking from an after-hours chore into a live instructional tool that students themselves were invested in.

Additionally, I built a small group creator which tracked students working on the same skills and close to proficiency, as well as recommended peer tutor groups based on students who had already mastered the material.

Custom Platform

Creating Desmos Academy

Texas provides every STAAR tester with the Desmos graphing calculator, yet most students use a fraction of its power. Skills like running a linear regression from two table points can turn multi-step STAAR problems into 30-second verifications, but only if students have practiced the moves until they are automatic. I built Desmos Academy, a gamified, self-paced learning management system, to teach exactly those skills to my 7th grade students testing on 8th grade standards.

The platform is a custom HTML application with a Google Apps Script and Google Sheets backend. Students log in with their student ID and progress through sequential modules covering slope and rate of change, writing equations in y = mx + b form, solving equations with variables on both sides, and additional concepts, each following a consistent structure: a worked example using actual STAAR items alongside Desmos calculator screenshots, scaffolded practice opportunities, and a Check Your Understanding checkpoint requiring 100% mastery to advance. The design leans into an aspirational academic aesthetic, complete with a graduation-capped brain mascot, and students who complete all modules "graduate" with a personalized digital diploma and confetti celebration.

Behind the scenes, the Apps Script backend logs every student action in real time: logins, practice completions, every checkpoint attempt including failures, and graduation status, all visible to me through an organized tracking spreadsheet. I also built integrity features directly into the system, including automated flagging of rapid guessing behavior so I could intervene with students clicking through rather than thinking. Desmos Academy connected to my broader grading ecosystem as well, with graduation earning students points through the Raise My Grade system on their progress portals. The result was a self-running review resource that taught high-leverage calculator fluency while generating the participation data I needed to target my in-person support.
